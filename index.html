Research

Electronic nanodevices and nanosensors

Two-dimensional semiconductors such as MoS2, MoSe2 and GaSe are thin, transparent and need little power, which makes them strong candidates to replace silicon in next-generation sensors. My work covers their optical properties, thickness control and photodetector fabrication.

  • Showed that laser-induced etching removes MoS2 one atomic layer at a time, giving atomic-resolution thickness control.
  • Explained the red-shift of the A exciton in monolayer MoS2 by a laser-induced Urbach tail.
  • Built back-gated field-effect phototransistors from few-layer MoSe2 and GaSe.

Internet of Medical Things: micro- and nanorobots for point-of-care diagnosis

Magnetic particles can be moved wirelessly through a fluid, which opens the way to diagnostics, biosensing, targeted therapy and single-particle drug delivery. Smaller robots are limited by fabrication complexity, so I work on simple designs.

  • Microrobots made of two self-assembled superparamagnetic particles (2.8 µm Dynabeads), driven by a rotating field from three pairs of electromagnetic coils. Speed rises with robot length and field frequency.
  • A vision-based LabVIEW program controls speed and direction, automatically or manually.
  • Soft nanorobots on a PVA/PLLA polymer nanosheet, steered by a 3D rotating field.
  • A portable fluorescent microscope, designed in Fusion 360 with three-axis coils, that detects biotin magnetic nanoparticles on the nanosheet.

Agri-IoT for smart, sustainable farming

Remote monitoring lets farmers follow plant growth and quality without being on site. Aerial and ground data can be analysed with AI to catch abnormalities and pests early and to target pesticide use. My prototype is an IoT hydroponic system built around a Raspberry Pi, reachable from phone, tablet and PC.

  • Chemical-route synthesis of thin-film solar cells.
